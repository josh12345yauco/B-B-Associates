How Long Does a Full Kitchen Renovation Take? A PA Contractor's Honest Guide

September 2024 · 5 min read
Home › Journal › How Long Does a Full Kitchen Renovation Take? A PA Contractor's Honest Guide

One of the most common questions we get during initial consultations: "How long will my kitchen take?" The honest answer is nuanced — but the framework below will help you set realistic expectations for any kitchen renovation in Pennsylvania, from a straightforward refresh to a full architectural transformation.

Phase 1: Design and Permitting (3–8 Weeks Before Construction)

Before a single wall comes down, you'll need to complete design, select all materials, and in most Montgomery County and Bucks County municipalities, pull a building permit. Permit timelines vary: Whitpain Township might take 2 weeks, while some boroughs take 4–6 weeks. B&B handles all permitting as part of our process — but factor this into your overall timeline.

This phase is also where the most important cost and schedule decisions get locked. The more thoroughly you finalize selections now — cabinetry line, countertop slab, tile, fixtures, hardware — the more predictable everything downstream becomes. Our custom cabinetry guide and countertop material guide are designed to help you make these choices with confidence.

Phase 2: Construction (4–10 Weeks)

A straightforward kitchen renovation — new cabinetry, countertops, tile, and fixtures with no structural changes — typically takes 4–6 weeks of active construction. Projects involving wall removal, plumbing relocation, or electrical panel upgrades realistically run 7–10 weeks.

Here's what usually extends timelines: material lead times. Custom cabinetry from premium manufacturers like Kraftmaid, Showplace, or Medallion can take 6–10 weeks to fabricate. We order materials before demo begins so construction isn't waiting on cabinets.

A Week-by-Week Look at Construction

While every project differs, a typical construction sequence for a mid-size kitchen looks like this:

  • Week 1 — Demolition & rough-in: Old kitchen comes out; plumbing, electrical, and any structural work begins.
  • Weeks 2–3 — Rough inspections, drywall, flooring prep: Systems are inspected, walls are closed, and substrates are prepared.
  • Weeks 3–4 — Cabinetry installation: The kitchen starts to take shape. Precise, level installation here determines how everything else fits.
  • Week 4–5 — Countertop templating & fabrication: Stone is templated only after cabinets are set, then fabricated off-site (about 1–2 weeks).
  • Weeks 5–6 — Backsplash, fixtures, appliances, finishes: Tile goes up, plumbing and electrical are trimmed out, appliances are set.
  • Final week — Punch list & walkthrough: Hardware, touch-ups, and a detailed final review.

The countertop gap — the week or two between cabinet installation and stone delivery — surprises many homeowners. It's unavoidable because stone can only be templated once the cabinets are physically in place. A good contractor sequences other work into that window so momentum never fully stops.

What Can Delay a Kitchen Project?

  • Late material changes (changing your countertop selection after it's been ordered)
  • Unexpected conditions (knob-and-tube wiring, structural issues discovered during demo)
  • Permit delays (especially common in spring and summer)
  • Custom fabrication backlogs

Structural projects deserve special mention. When a layout requires raising a floor, relocating load-bearing walls, or redesigning a window system, the timeline naturally extends. Our Structural Raised-Floor Kitchen ran about 12 weeks because half the floor had to be raised to a single level — a reminder that ambitious results take time. By contrast, a clean cosmetic renovation like our White Shaker Subway Kitchen can wrap in around six weeks. Our breakdown of open-concept structural costs explains what's involved when walls come down.

The Realistic Total Timeline

From your first consultation to your final walkthrough: plan for 12–20 weeks for a standard kitchen renovation, 20–28 weeks for a complex architectural transformation. The best thing you can do is start the conversation early — especially if you're targeting a spring completion before summer entertaining season.

How to Keep Your Project On Schedule

Homeowners have more influence over the timeline than they often realize. The two biggest levers are decisiveness and early ordering. Finalize every selection before demolition, resist the temptation to change orders once materials are in production, and trust your design-build team to sequence the trades. Choosing the right firm matters here too — a contractor who communicates clearly and orders materials proactively will simply finish faster. Our guide to choosing a remodeling contractor covers what to look for.

Living Through a Kitchen Renovation

For most families, the hardest part of a kitchen remodel isn't the cost — it's living without a kitchen for several weeks. A good contractor plans for that reality. We help clients set up a temporary kitchen (a microwave, a portable cooktop, a refrigerator relocated to the dining room or garage), establish clear dust barriers, and protect the pathways through your home. Knowing the realistic duration up front — and having a contractor who respects your home during the process — turns a stressful disruption into a manageable, finite project.

Seasonal Timing in the Philadelphia Suburbs

When you start matters more than most homeowners expect. Spring and early summer are peak season across Montgomery County and Bucks County, which means permit offices are busier and the best trades book out further. Starting design in late winter to hit a spring construction window — or beginning a project in late summer for fall completion — often means smoother permitting and more scheduling flexibility. If your goal is to host for the holidays, the conversation needs to start by late summer, not October.

How Material Choices Drive Lead Time

The single biggest variable in a kitchen timeline usually isn't the construction — it's how long your materials take to arrive. Full-custom cabinetry can run 8–12 weeks to fabricate; semi-custom lines are faster. Specialty stone slabs may need to be sourced and inspected. Imported tile and certain appliances can carry their own lead times. Because we order long-lead items before demolition begins, your construction phase isn't held hostage to a back-ordered cabinet. Our cabinetry guide explains the trade-offs between stock, semi-custom, and full-custom in detail.

The Role of Inspections

Permitted work includes inspections at specific milestones — typically after rough electrical and plumbing, and again at completion. Inspections protect you: they verify the work meets code and was done correctly. They can also affect the schedule, since the next phase can't proceed until the inspection passes. As your design-build firm, we schedule and coordinate every inspection with the township so the project keeps moving and nothing stalls waiting on a phone call you didn't know you had to make.

Why Rushing a Kitchen Costs More

It's tempting to push for the fastest possible completion, but the kitchens that go fastest are rarely the ones done best. Skipping proper material lead time means substituting lesser products; rushing tile or cabinetry installation invites the kind of flaws that have to be redone. The most efficient project is a well-sequenced one, not a hurried one. This is also why the cheapest, fastest bid is so often a false economy — a theme we explore in our guides to true kitchen cost and choosing the right contractor.

Structural Projects Take Longer — and That's Okay

If your vision involves removing walls or correcting a split-level layout, build extra time into your expectations. Our Structural Raised-Floor Kitchen required raising half the floor to a single level and took roughly 12 weeks of construction; our Open-Concept Cherry Amber Kitchen involved full wall removal. Ambitious results are worth the additional weeks — and our open-concept cost guide explains exactly what structural work entails.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a full kitchen renovation take? Plan for 12–20 weeks from first consultation to final walkthrough for a standard renovation, and 20–28 weeks for a complex architectural transformation. Active on-site construction is typically 4–10 weeks.

What takes the longest in a kitchen remodel? Usually material lead times, especially custom cabinetry (8–12 weeks to fabricate). We order long-lead items before demolition so construction isn't waiting on them.

Can I stay in my home during the renovation? Almost always. We set up a temporary kitchen, establish dust barriers, and protect pathways. Knowing the realistic, finite timeline up front makes the disruption manageable.

When should I start planning? Earlier than you'd think — peak spring/summer demand fills permit offices and trade calendars. What Sets a Predictable Timeline Apart

The contractors who finish on schedule are rarely the fastest talkers — they're the most organized planners. A predictable kitchen timeline is the product of disciplines that happen long before demolition: finalizing every selection during the design phase, ordering long-lead materials early, sequencing the trades so each is ready when the previous finishes, and coordinating inspections proactively with the township. When those things are handled well, the project moves in a steady rhythm and the end date you were promised is the end date you get.

When they're handled poorly, the opposite happens: cabinets arrive late because nobody ordered them in time, the tile setter shows up before the drywall is ready, the inspection wasn't scheduled and the next phase stalls for a week. These aren't bad-luck delays — they're planning failures, and they're avoidable. This is one of the most important things to evaluate when you're hiring, which is why we wrote a full guide to choosing a remodeling contractor without getting burned.
