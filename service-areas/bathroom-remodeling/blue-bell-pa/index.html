What a Blue Bell primary bath was built with

The 1980s–2000s colonials that fill Whitpain and Montgomery Township came with a primary suite drawn to a price: a corner garden tub under a window, a small framed shower with a curb, a cultured-marble double vanity, a fan that may vent into the attic, and a lot of square footage used badly. The 1960s–70s colonials have the opposite problem, a hall bath and a primary bath so small that the shower was an afterthought.

In the newer homes the project is almost always a reallocation of space: the tub deck comes out, a 60- to 72-inch walk-in shower with a bench and frameless glass goes in its place, and a freestanding tub is added only if the homeowner will actually use it. In the older colonials the project is often a closet or a hallway linen cabinet giving up its footprint so the bathroom can finally hold a real shower and a vanity with storage.

What Blue Bell bathroom projects run into

Compared with the stone homes we work in on the Main Line, Blue Bell houses are straightforward: drywall, PVC drains, 200-amp panels. The items we still plan for are the fan venting to the exterior, a shower rebuilt on a true waterproofing system rather than the original mud-set or drywall backing, a subfloor checked for the settling that shows up under large-format tile, and any plumbing move over a finished ceiling, which we price as its own line.

A remodel involving plumbing and electrical is permitted through Whitpain Township, Montgomery Township, or North Wales Borough depending on your address, and inspected at rough-in. We file the applications and schedule the inspections inside our scope. Most Blue Bell bathrooms take about 5–7 weeks of construction once the vanity, stone, and glass ordered during the design phase are on site.

Investment, resale, and the upgrades that matter

Hall baths done right in Blue Bell run $38,000–$55,000. Primary baths with a custom vanity, expanded shower, frameless glass, and heated floors run $55,000–$85,000. Spa suites with steam, a soaking tub, and large-format porcelain throughout run $85,000–$100,000+. In a Blue Bell colonial the primary bath and the kitchen are the two rooms buyers pay for, so a well-executed bathroom is also one of the better resale investments in the township.

Heated floors are the upgrade we would never leave out in a Pennsylvania winter. A curbless shower with a linear drain is the request from homeowners planning to stay in the house for decades. The consultation is free, the estimate is itemized before any work begins, and design selections can be finalized at the Ambler showroom once it opens in Winter 2026/2027.
