Why the 1950s and 60s bathrooms around Ambler keep failing

A large share of the housing stock in Ambler and Upper Dublin is 1920s–1960s construction, and the bathrooms in those homes share a set of problems. The exhaust fan, if there is one, often vents into the attic instead of outside. The shower wall was built on drywall or old mud-set tile with no waterproof membrane. There is frequently a window inside the shower that has not been sealed properly in decades. The result is a bathroom that grows mold no matter how often it is cleaned, and a homeowner who has replaced the fan twice without fixing anything.

Those are not cosmetic problems and they do not have cosmetic fixes. When we remodel a bathroom in one of these homes we vent the fan through the roof or the wall to the exterior, build the shower on a true waterproofing system, and rebuild the window opening so water has nowhere to go. That is the difference between a bathroom that looks new for two years and one that stays dry for thirty.

What we plan for in Ambler Borough and Upper Dublin homes

In the Borough's older twins and colonials, the bathroom is usually small, the drain stack is cast iron, and the floor has settled enough that a large-format tile will telegraph every dip unless the subfloor is rebuilt. We assess all of that during the free in-home consultation so the estimate accounts for it. Moving a toilet or expanding a shower into a closet is often the best money spent in these houses, because the original layout was drawn for a family that bathed once a week.

A bathroom remodel that involves plumbing, electrical, and often a window change is permitted through Ambler Borough or Upper Dublin Township, and both inspect the rough-in before the walls close. We handle the applications and the inspection scheduling inside our scope. Most Ambler bathrooms take about 5–7 weeks of construction once materials are on site; older homes can add a week if the stack or the framing needs more than expected.

Investment and where the money goes

A hall or guest bath done right in Ambler, with proper venting, a waterproofed tiled shower, a new vanity, and updated lighting, runs $38,000–$55,000. Primary baths with a custom vanity, expanded walk-in shower with frameless glass, and heated floors run $55,000–$85,000. Spa suites with steam, a freestanding tub, and large-format porcelain throughout run $85,000–$100,000+. Ranges reflect completed projects and depend on square footage, plumbing moves, and finish level.

Heated floors are the upgrade we would never leave out in a Pennsylvania winter, and in a small Ambler bathroom the electric radiant mat is a modest line item. Spend on the shower waterproofing and the vanity; those are the two things you cannot upgrade later without starting over.
