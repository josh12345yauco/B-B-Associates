The Short Answer

Kitchen Remodel Cost

In 2026, a kitchen remodel by B&B Associates in Montgomery County, Bucks County or on the Main Line runs from about $35,000 for a semi-custom refresh to $100,000 and beyond for a full architectural transformation. Most of our clients land between $60,000 and $100,000: full custom cabinetry, premium countertops, new lighting and some layout change. Every estimate is itemized line by line before work begins, and the in-home consultation that produces it is free.

Investment Guide

Kitchen Remodel Cost by Tier

Foundation

$35,000 – $60,000

Semi-custom cabinets, quartz counters, tile backsplash, new fixtures and lighting. Layout stays where it is.

Premium

$60,000 – $100,000

Full custom cabinetry, premium stone, lighting design and moderate structural change, such as widening an opening or moving an island.

Signature

$100,000+

Architectural-level transformation: walls removed, plumbing and gas relocated, bespoke cabinetry throughout, smart-home integration. Full luxury kitchens with structural work commonly reach $150,000+.

Ranges reflect typical completed B&B projects and vary with square footage, material selections and structural scope. A precise number comes from your free in-home consultation.

What Actually Drives the Price

Cabinetry is almost always the largest single line on a kitchen estimate, and it is the line with the widest range. Premium semi-custom cabinetry covers most luxury kitchens beautifully at 30–50% less than full custom; full custom earns its price when you need non-standard sizes, unusual wood species like hickory or walnut, or inset doors with furniture-grade details.

The second driver is what moves. Keeping the sink, range and refrigerator where they are keeps plumbing, gas and electrical work to a minimum. Relocating any of them, or opening a wall to the dining room, adds trades, permits and inspections, and that is the difference between the Foundation and Signature tiers more often than the finishes are.

Third is the house itself. Ambler's Victorian-era homes and the 1920s–1950s colonials in Fort Washington and Flourtown often need a panel upgrade, new circuits and plaster repair once the walls open. Bucks County stone homes in Doylestown, New Hope and Newtown bring their own surprises. We assess for these before we quote, which is why our estimates rarely change after demolition.

What Is Included in a B&B Estimate

Design, permits, materials, labor and project management under one contract. There is no separate designer's invoice and no permit runaround: we file with Ambler Borough, Upper Dublin, Whitpain, Newtown Township, Doylestown Borough or whichever municipality governs your address, and we coordinate every inspection.

Because we buy through 60+ supplier partners, cabinetry, stone, tile and fixtures come in at trade pricing, typically 5–35% below retail. That is built into the estimate you see, not added later.

What is not included is anything we have not seen. If demolition reveals a rotted subfloor or knob-and-tube wiring, we stop, show you, and price the fix as a separate line before touching it. No surprise costs, no change-order games.

How Long a Kitchen Remodel Takes

Plan for roughly 12–20 weeks from first design meeting to final walkthrough. Design and selections take 3–8 weeks, permitting 2–4 weeks depending on the township, and construction 4–10 weeks. Custom cabinetry lead times are the usual reason a schedule stretches, so we order cabinets before demolition, not after.

You can live in the house through most of it. We set up a temporary kitchen, seal the work zone, and keep a predictable daily schedule so the disruption has an end date you can plan around.

Ways to Control the Number

Keep the plumbing where it is. Choose premium semi-custom cabinetry unless you truly need custom. Put the budget into the surfaces you touch every day, counters and hardware, and be practical about the ones you don't. Decide on everything before construction starts; mid-project changes cost more than the same decision made on paper.

And get an itemized estimate from anyone you consider. A single-number quote hides where the money goes and makes it impossible to compare two contractors honestly.
